# Chapter 7 — Academic Integrity and Disciplinary Code

## §7.1 Plagiarism

Plagiarism is defined as the submission of another person's words, ideas,
data, or work product as one's own without appropriate attribution, in any
assignment, project report, thesis, or other academic submission. All
written submissions above 2,000 words are routed through the university's
plagiarism-detection software, and a similarity index above twenty-five
percent (25%), excluding properly quoted and cited material, is referred
to the Disciplinary Committee for review under §7.3. A first plagiarism
finding typically results in a zero mark for the submission concerned; a
second finding across a student's academic career typically results in
suspension for one semester, subject to the Committee's discretion under
§7.4.

## §7.2 Examination Malpractice

Examination malpractice includes possession of unauthorized material
during an examination, copying from another candidate, impersonation, and
any attempt to communicate with another person during the examination by
any means. An invigilator who observes suspected malpractice shall seize
the relevant material or device, record the incident in writing, and refer
the matter to the Disciplinary Committee under §7.3. The candidate is
permitted to complete the remainder of the examination under observation,
without prejudice to the outcome of the disciplinary proceeding.

## §7.3 Disciplinary Committee Procedure

The Disciplinary Committee, comprising the Dean of Academic Affairs, one
faculty representative from the student's department, and one
representative of the Dean of Student Affairs, reviews all referrals under
§7.1 and §7.2. The student concerned is given written notice of the
allegation and an opportunity to respond in writing or in person within
ten working days. The Committee's decision, along with reasons, is
communicated to the student in writing and is recorded in the student's
academic file. Proceedings of the Committee are confidential and are not
disclosed to any party other than the student concerned and university
officials with a legitimate need to know.

## §7.4 Penalties and Sanctions

Sanctions available to the Disciplinary Committee range from a written
warning, to a zero mark on the concerned submission or examination, to
suspension for one or more semesters, to expulsion from the university in
cases of repeated or particularly severe violation. In determining the
sanction, the Committee shall have regard to the severity of the
violation, whether it is a first or repeat occurrence, and any mitigating
circumstances placed on record by the student. A student found responsible
for an integrity violation may, within fifteen working days of the
decision, submit an appeal in accordance with §8.4.
