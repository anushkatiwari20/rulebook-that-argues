# Chapter 10 — IT and Network Acceptable Use Policy

## §10.1 Scope and Account Issuance

Every registered student is issued a university email account and network
credentials upon enrollment, which remain active for the duration of the
student's programme and for ninety days following graduation or
withdrawal, after which the account is deactivated and its contents are
permanently deleted. These credentials provide access to the university
Wi-Fi network, the student portal, the Library Management System
described in Chapter 9, and prescribed academic software. Credentials are
personal and non-transferable.

## §10.2 Acceptable Use of the Network

The university network and computing resources are provided for academic
and administrative purposes. Use of the network for commercial activity
unrelated to university-approved work, for hosting or distributing
copyrighted material without authorization, or for any activity that
violates applicable law is prohibited. Bandwidth-intensive recreational
use, such as large-scale media streaming or file-sharing, may be
throttled by the Network Office during periods of high academic demand
without prior notice.

## §10.3 Prohibited Conduct

Attempting to gain unauthorized access to another user's account, to
university administrative systems, or to any system outside the
university network from within it, is strictly prohibited and is treated
as a serious disciplinary matter under Chapter 7, regardless of whether
the attempt succeeds. Introducing malicious software onto university
systems, whether intentionally or through gross negligence such as
disabling required security software, is likewise a disciplinary matter.
The Network Office monitors network traffic for security purposes and may
suspend access immediately, without prior notice, where a credible threat
to network integrity is identified, subject to subsequent review.

## §10.4 University Email Usage

All official academic communication, including notices under §1.5,
examination schedules, and grievance correspondence under §8.2, is sent
exclusively to a student's university email account, and a student is
deemed to have received such communication once it is delivered to that
account, whether or not it is actually read. Students are responsible for
checking their university email regularly and may not rely on
communication to a personal email address as a substitute for university
correspondence. Auto-forwarding of university email to a personal account
is permitted but is done entirely at the student's own risk regarding
delivery failures.

## §10.5 Software and Licensing

The university provides access to a defined set of licensed academic
software, listed on the IT Services page of the student portal, for use
on university-owned computers in laboratories and, where the license
permits, on personally owned devices for the duration of enrollment.
Installation of unlicensed or pirated software on university-owned
equipment is prohibited and may result in suspension of computing
privileges in addition to any other applicable disciplinary consequence.
Students requiring specialized software not on the approved list may
submit a request to IT Services, which is evaluated on a case-by-case
basis subject to budget and licensing availability.

## §10.6 Data Backup and Liability

Students are solely responsible for maintaining independent backups of
their academic work, including assignments, project files, and research
data. While the university takes reasonable measures to maintain the
availability and integrity of shared systems such as the Library
Management System and the student portal, the university is not liable
for loss of data stored on personal devices, university laboratory
computers, or cloud storage services accessed through university
credentials, and no claim for extension of a submission deadline will be
entertained solely on the ground of data loss.
