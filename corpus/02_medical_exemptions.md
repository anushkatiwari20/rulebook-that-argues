# Chapter 2 — Medical Exemptions and Leave

## §2.1 Definition and Scope of Medical Leave

Medical leave refers to any period of absence from scheduled academic
activity that arises directly from a student's own diagnosed illness,
injury, hospitalization, or a medical procedure, and that is supported by
documentation issued by a registered medical practitioner. Medical leave
does not extend to the illness of a family member, a dependent, or any
other person, however serious, save where the student is the sole
registered caregiver and has obtained prior written approval from the Dean
of Student Affairs under the exceptional-circumstances provision described
in §2.4.

## §2.2 Medical Exemption Attendance Threshold

A student whose attendance in a course has fallen short of the
requirement set out in §1.4 on account of medical leave under §2.1 may
apply for a medical exemption. Where the application is supported by valid
medical certification covering the period of absence and is submitted
through the Medical Exemption Portal within fifteen calendar days of the
student's return to classes, a student with attendance not less than
sixty-five percent (65%) in the affected course shall be deemed eligible
to appear in the semester-end examination notwithstanding §1.4. The
medical exemption is evaluated by the course instructor in consultation
with the University Health Centre and is recorded separately from any
condonation granted under §1.6.

## §2.3 Documentation and Certification Requirements

Medical certificates submitted in support of an exemption under §2.2 must
be issued by a practitioner registered with a recognized medical council,
must state the specific dates of incapacity, and must be submitted in
original or digitally attested form. Certificates issued by practitioners
not recognized under Indian medical regulations, or certificates that do
not specify exact dates, will be rejected. Self-certification, or
certification by a practitioner who is a family member of the student, is
not accepted under any circumstance. The University Health Centre reserves
the right to independently verify any certificate submitted.

## §2.4 Extended Medical Leave and Semester Freeze

A student who is unable to attend classes for a continuous period
exceeding thirty (30) calendar days due to a medical condition may apply
to the Dean of Student Affairs for a "semester freeze," under which the
semester is withdrawn without academic or financial penalty beyond
non-refundable components described in §5.3, and the student is permitted
to re-register for the same semester in a subsequent academic term. An
application for semester freeze must be accompanied by a certificate from
a specialist physician and must be filed no later than the last date of
the semester's teaching period. Semester freeze is not available for
absences shorter than thirty days; such shorter absences are handled
exclusively through the exemption process in §2.2.

## §2.5 Mental Health Support Provisions

The University Health Centre operates a confidential counselling service
for students experiencing psychological or emotional distress. Students
may self-refer or be referred by a faculty member. Attendance relief
connected to a mental health condition is available strictly through the
medical certification route described in §2.1 through §2.3, where a
registered psychiatrist or clinical psychologist issues documentation
equivalent to a medical certificate; the counselling service itself does
not issue attendance-relevant certification, and informal counselling
attendance records cannot be submitted as a substitute for the medical
certification required under §2.3.
