# Chapter 11 — Anti-Ragging and Campus Safety

## §11.1 Anti-Ragging Policy

Ragging in any form, whether physical, verbal, or psychological, is
strictly prohibited on campus, in hostels, and at any university-
sponsored event, in accordance with applicable national anti-ragging
regulations. Ragging includes any act that causes annoyance, hardship,
psychological harm, or embarrassment to a student, whether committed by a
senior student, a group, or otherwise. All incoming students are required
to submit a signed anti-ragging undertaking at the time of admission as a
condition of enrollment.

## §11.2 Anti-Ragging Committee

The university maintains an Anti-Ragging Committee, chaired by the Dean of
Student Affairs and including faculty, non-teaching staff, and student
representatives, which operates independently of the Disciplinary
Committee described in Chapter 7. Complaints may be filed through the
Anti-Ragging Helpline, which operates twenty-four hours a day, or in
writing to any Committee member. The Committee is empowered to recommend
immediate interim measures, including relocation of the complainant or
the accused within hostel accommodation, pending completion of a full
inquiry.

## §11.3 Campus Security

Campus Security personnel are stationed at all entry and exit points and
patrol the campus and hostel premises at all hours. Visitors to hostel
premises must register at the security desk and are permitted only in
designated common areas during visiting hours published by the Hostel
Office; visitors are not permitted in student rooms without the prior
written consent of the Warden. Any student witnessing a security concern,
including unauthorized persons on campus, is expected to report it
immediately to Campus Security.

## §11.4 Emergency Medical Response

The University Health Centre operates an emergency response service
during teaching hours, and Campus Security maintains a list of
on-call medical contacts for after-hours emergencies. In a medical
emergency, Campus Security or the University Health Centre will arrange
transport to the nearest partner hospital; the cost of emergency
transport is borne by the university, while subsequent treatment costs
are the responsibility of the student or their insurance, consistent with
the university's group medical insurance scheme where the student is
enrolled in it.

## §11.5 Fire and Evacuation Procedures

Fire drills are conducted in all hostel blocks and academic buildings at
least once per semester, and attendance at a scheduled fire drill for
one's own residential block is mandatory for hostel residents. Tampering
with fire safety equipment, including extinguishers and smoke detectors,
is treated as a serious disciplinary matter under Chapter 7 regardless of
whether an actual fire results. Evacuation routes are posted on every
floor of every hostel block and academic building, and students are
expected to familiarize themselves with the route nearest to their
regular classroom and hostel room.

## §11.6 Lost and Found

Items found on campus, in academic buildings, or in common hostel areas
should be handed to the nearest Campus Security desk, which maintains a
logged Lost and Found register. A student who has lost an item may check
the register in person or query it through the Campus Security helpline.
Unclaimed items are held for ninety days from the date they are logged,
after which they are donated to a charity partner selected by the Dean of
Student Affairs. The university accepts no liability for items lost on
campus and does not compensate students for the value of unclaimed or
undeliverable items.
