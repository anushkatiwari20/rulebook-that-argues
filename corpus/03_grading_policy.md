# Chapter 3 — Examination Conduct and Grading Policy

## §3.1 Examination Conduct and Malpractice Reference

All students appearing for a semester-end examination must carry their
university identity card and be seated at least fifteen minutes before the
scheduled start time. Electronic devices of any kind, including
smartwatches, are prohibited in the examination hall unless expressly
permitted for a specific accommodation. Any suspected use of unfair means
is referred to the Disciplinary Committee under the procedure described in
Chapter 7 of this handbook, and the outcome of that proceeding, not this
chapter, governs the consequences for the student concerned.

## §3.2 Grading Scale and Grade Points

Bannerworth University follows a ten-point grading scale. A score of
ninety percent (90%) and above is graded O (Outstanding, 10 grade points);
80–89% is graded A+ (9 points); 70–79% is graded A (8 points); 60–69% is
graded B+ (7 points); 50–59% is graded B (6 points); 45–49% is graded C (5
points); 40–44% is graded P (4 points, the minimum passing grade); and
below 40% is graded F (0 points, a fail requiring the course to be
repeated). The Semester Grade Point Average is computed as the credit-
weighted mean of grade points across all courses registered in a semester.

## §3.3 Re-evaluation and Revaluation Procedure

A student who is dissatisfied with the marks awarded in a semester-end
examination may apply for revaluation, under which the answer script is
re-checked by the original examiner for totaling errors and unmarked
answers, or for re-evaluation, under which the script is assessed afresh
by an independent examiner. Applications must be submitted through the
Examination Cell within ten working days of the declaration of results
under §3.4. Revaluation and re-evaluation may result in the mark being
increased, decreased, or remaining unchanged, and the revised mark, once
declared, is final and not subject to further challenge through this
procedure.

## §3.4 Declaration of Results

Results for semester-end examinations are declared by the Examination
Cell within twenty-one calendar days of the last examination in that
semester, and are published on the student portal. A physical or digital
grade card is issued to each student within thirty days of declaration.
Provisional grade cards may be issued earlier for students requiring
documentation for internship or placement purposes, upon written request
to the Examination Cell.

## §3.5 Backlog and Supplementary Examinations

A student who fails a course, or who was debarred from an examination
under §1.4 and did not obtain a condonation under §1.6 or a medical
exemption under §2.2, carries the course forward as a "backlog." Backlog
courses may be cleared either by repeating the course in a subsequent
regular semester in which it is offered, or, where the university
schedules one, by appearing in a supplementary examination held before the
start of the next academic year. A student may carry a maximum of four
backlog courses at any point in their programme; a student exceeding this
limit is placed under academic probation and referred to the Dean of
Academic Affairs.
